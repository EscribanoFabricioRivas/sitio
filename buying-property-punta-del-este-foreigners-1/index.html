The role of the Escribano Público and why the buyer selects the notary in Uruguay

Buyer-side legal counsel licensed by the Supreme Court of Justice

Equal ownership rights and independent closing control

Foreign individuals and entities acquire property in Uruguay with the exact same ownership rights as Uruguayan citizens. Because the buyer assumes ownership and payment risk, your chosen Escribano Público works exclusively to verify the property's legal standing, coordinate registry searches, confirm tax clearances, and draft the final transfer deed (escritura pública de compraventa).

Reservation contract (boleto de reserva) and notarial escrow custody of the deposit

Key legal clauses in a Uruguayan boleto de reserva

In Uruguay, the boleto de reserva is a binding contract that sets the purchase price, property unit or parcel identification, closing timeline, inventory of furnishings when applicable, and penalty provisions. Reviewing the draft before signing ensures the wording protects your position as buyer.

Notarial escrow custody of the reservation deposit

Standard reservations involve a deposit (customarily 10% of the purchase price) delivered to the buyer's Escribano Público as escrow custodian (depositario), keeping funds safeguarded under strict release conditions. Funds transfer to the seller only once full title and registry due diligence confirms clean marketable title and verified municipal and tax clearances, with clear refund mechanisms written directly into the agreement.

Full title and registry due diligence across public registries and tax authorities

Title chain, Actos Personales, and Catastro verification

Before closing, your Escribano Público examines the complete historical chain of deeds and obtains official registry certificates. This includes searches at the Registro de la Propiedad Inmueble (mortgages, easements, attachments, and prior transfers), Registro Nacional de Actos Personales (liens, judicial inhibitions, marital property regimes, and insolvency checks on the sellers), and Dirección Nacional de Catastro (cédula catastral confirming official cadastral valuation and parcel boundaries).

Municipal taxes, BPS construction status, Primary Tax, and HOA gastos comunes

Due diligence also covers Intendencia de Maldonado real estate tax (contribución inmobiliaria), Impuesto de Enseñanza Primaria (Primary Education Tax), Banco de Previsión Social (BPS construction and social security regularization for new builds or renovations), and written clearance from the building administrator confirming zero arrears on gastos comunes (HOA fees).

Frequently Asked Questions on Buying Property in Punta del Este

Can non-resident foreigners buy property in Punta del Este in their own name?

Yes. Under Uruguayan law, foreign individuals hold the same property rights as Uruguayan citizens and can acquire real estate in Punta del Este and Maldonado in their personal name using a valid passport. Uruguayan residency and a local bank account remain optional for property ownership, with closing funds transferred under standard international banking compliance.

During a 4-to-15-day trip, what can be completed before flying home?

During a 4-to-15-day visit, you can coordinate prior document pre-review via WhatsApp (+598 99 431 185), sign a buyer-reviewed reservation contract (boleto de reserva), place the reservation deposit in notarial escrow custody, and execute a local Uruguayan Power of Attorney (poder especial) before your return flight so your closing moves forward smoothly on schedule.

Why is signing a Power of Attorney in Uruguay faster than sending one from abroad?

A Power of Attorney executed directly before a Uruguayan Escribano Público in Punta del Este is drafted in Spanish under local registry standards and is ready for immediate use in Uruguay. By contrast, a Power of Attorney signed abroad requires foreign notarization, a Hague Convention Apostille from the issuing country, courier delivery to Uruguay, a sworn Spanish translation by a licensed Traductor Público, and notarial protocolization before it can be used at closing.

What does full title and registry due diligence check before closing?

Full title and registry due diligence verifies the property's complete deed chain and official certificates across Registro de la Propiedad Inmueble, Registro Nacional de Actos Personales (liens, attachments, inhibitions, and seller legal capacity), Dirección Nacional de Catastro, BPS construction compliance, Intendencia de Maldonado municipal taxes, Impuesto de Enseñanza Primaria, and building HOA (gastos comunes) standing.

What happens to my reservation deposit if due diligence reveals a lien, debt, or unpaid tax?

When the reservation contract includes standard buyer-protective clauses drafted by your Escribano Público, the deposit remains in notarial escrow custody pending cure of the title defect or clear tax certification. If the seller fails to clear a lien, municipal debt, or registry defect within the agreed cure period, the contract establishes the immediate refund of your reservation deposit under the agreed terms.
