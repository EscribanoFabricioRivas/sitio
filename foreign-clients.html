Frequently Asked Questions · Foreign Clients

Practical answers on Uruguayan notarial practice in Punta del Este.

What is the difference between an Escribano Público in Uruguay and a Notary Public in the US or Europe?

In Uruguay, an Escribano Público is a university-trained legal practitioner licensed by the Supreme Court of Justice (SCJ Matrícula 17058/5) and vested with public faith. While a US Notary Public primarily verifies identity and witnesses signatures, a Uruguayan Escribano Público conducts full title and registry due diligence, checks tax and municipal clearances, drafts the purchase deed, and registers ownership in the public land registry. In real estate transactions, the Escribano Público serves as the buyer's independent legal counsel and closing officer.

Who chooses the Escribano Público when buying property in Punta del Este?

Under Uruguayan real estate practice, the buyer selects and appoints the Escribano Público. Because the buyer commits capital and assumes long-term ownership risk, your chosen Escribano Público independently reviews the reservation contract, examines the complete title history and registry certificates, confirms tax and building clearance, and drafts the final public deed (escritura de compraventa) to safeguard your acquisition.

Can I review the reservation contract and sign a Power of Attorney in Uruguay before flying home?

Yes. During a short visit to Punta del Este, you can send the draft reservation contract via WhatsApp (+598 99 431 185) for prior review before committing funds, and execute a Uruguayan Power of Attorney (poder) locally before flying home. Signing a local Power of Attorney directly before a Uruguayan Escribano Público gives your representative valid authority in Uruguay and saves the added time and cost of foreign notarization, a Hague Apostille, and a sworn Traductor Público translation.

Through prior document pre-review via WhatsApp (+598 99 431 185) before your signing appointment in Punta del Este, we confirm the exact text, receiving institution requirements, and formal wording so your signature certification is ready for signing on your scheduled day.

When is a notarial income certificate accepted vs. a Contador Público certificate (Migración residency, banks, and rental insurers)?

Dirección Nacional de Migración accepts a certificado notarial de ingresos issued by an Escribano Público to substantiate lawful means of living for legal residency under Law 18.250. For Uruguayan bank onboarding, the compliance team specifies on its written checklist whether it requires an Escribano Público certificate, a Contador Público statement, or both. For rental guarantee policies (seguro de garantía de alquiler) with insurers such as Porto Seguro, SURA, MAPFRE, or SBI, underwriting typically asks for a Contador Público certificate when income derives from local Uruguayan activity, while select insurers accept an Escribano Público certificate for foreign-income or asset-backed files.

Do foreign buyers need Uruguayan residency or a local bank account to buy property or certify documents?

Foreign buyers can purchase property in Punta del Este and certify signatures using a valid passport alone. Uruguayan residency and a local bank account remain optional for property acquisition or signature certification, and international buyers hold identical ownership rights to Uruguayan citizens under Uruguayan law, with closing funds wired under standard international banking compliance.
