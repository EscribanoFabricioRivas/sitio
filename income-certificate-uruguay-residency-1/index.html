Notarial Income Certificate in Uruguay for Residency and Rental Guarantees.

When applying for legal residency before Dirección Nacional de Migración, opening a bank account or arranging a rental guarantee in Punta del Este and Maldonado, foreign residents and remote workers are asked to prove their monthly income. Reviewing your supporting documents in advance ensures the certificate matches the exact wording required by the receiving institution.

Two distinct purposes

Residency before Migración vs. Rental Guarantee Insurers

In Uruguay, a notarial income certificate (certificado notarial de ingresos or proof of means of living) is issued by an Escribano Público based on verifiable supporting documentation. However, the wording and signatory requirements vary depending on who receives the document:

  • Dirección Nacional de Migración (Legal Residency): Accepts a notarial certificate of means of living issued by an Escribano Público based on foreign employment contracts, independent contractor agreements, invoices, bank statements, pensions or company distributions.
  • Rental Guarantee Insurers (Seguro de Garantía de Alquiler): Some Uruguayan insurers accept a notarial income certificate, while others specifically request an income certificate signed by a Uruguayan Public Accountant (Contador Público). Confirming the insurer requirement before drafting avoids paying twice.
  • Local Banks: Require clear traceability of funds, source of income and supporting statements aligned with anti-money laundering standards.

Remote contractors & foreign documents

Supporting documentation reviewed before drafting

Foreign professionals working remotely for companies abroad (independent contractors, foreign LLC or self-employed owners, or salaried remote employees) typically share digital copies first so the Escribano Público can confirm whether the file is complete and whether any document requires apostille or sworn translation:

  • Passport or valid Uruguayan identity document.
  • Bank statements (personal and business accounts) showing recurring incoming transfers.
  • Invoices, independent contractor agreements or employer confirmation letters.
  • Foreign company or self-employment registration certificates when income is billed through an entity abroad.
